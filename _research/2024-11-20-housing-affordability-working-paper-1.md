## Series Overview

This working paper series provides a comprehensive analysis of housing affordability in Houston, examining market dynamics, supply constraints, and potential policy interventions. The series is structured as follows:

**Installment 1** (This Paper): Establishes the empirical foundation by documenting housing affordability trends, supply and demand factors, and market characteristics.

**Installment 2** (Forthcoming): Analyzes regulatory factors, land use policies, and their impact on housing supply elasticity.

**Installment 3** (Forthcoming): Evaluates policy interventions including inclusionary zoning, subsidies, and supply-side reforms.

## Abstract

Houston presents a unique case in American urban housing markets. Despite rapid population growth and strong demand, the city has maintained relatively affordable housing compared to other major metropolitan areas. This installment examines the empirical trends in Houston's housing market from 2010-2024, documenting affordability metrics, supply responses, and the factors contributing to Houston's relatively elastic housing supply.

## 1. Introduction

Housing affordability has become a critical challenge in many American cities, with median home prices exceeding six times median household income in some metropolitan areas. Houston, however, has bucked this trend, maintaining more affordable housing despite being the fourth-largest city in the United States and experiencing substantial population growth.

Understanding Houston's housing market dynamics offers valuable lessons for urban policy and housing economics more broadly. This working paper series aims to:

1. Document Houston's housing affordability trends and compare them to peer cities
2. Analyze the supply and demand factors underlying Houston's market dynamics
3. Examine the role of regulatory environment and land use policies
4. Evaluate potential policy interventions for maintaining and enhancing affordability

### 1.1 Why Houston?

Houston is particularly interesting for several reasons:

- **Minimal Zoning**: Unlike most major U.S. cities, Houston lacks traditional zoning regulations
- **Abundant Land**: Geographic expansion is less constrained than in coastal cities
- **Strong Population Growth**: The metro area has grown by over 20% since 2010
- **Diverse Economy**: Reduced vulnerability to single-industry shocks
- **Natural Experiments**: Variation in regulations across the metro area

## 2. Data and Measurement

### 2.1 Data Sources

This analysis draws on multiple data sources:

- **Home Prices**: Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI), CoreLogic, and MLS data
- **Income Data**: American Community Survey (ACS), Bureau of Economic Analysis
- **Housing Supply**: Census Building Permits Survey, property tax records
- **Demographic Data**: ACS, Census Bureau population estimates
- **Economic Indicators**: Bureau of Labor Statistics, Houston Business Cycle Index

### 2.2 Key Metrics

I employ several standard measures of housing affordability:

**Price-to-Income Ratio** = Median Home Price / Median Household Income

**Affordability Index** = (Median Income / Qualifying Income at Median Price) × 100

**Rental Burden** = Share of households spending >30% of income on rent

## 3. Empirical Trends: 2010-2024

### 3.1 Home Price Evolution

Houston home prices have grown substantially over the study period, but at a slower rate than most major metropolitan areas:

| Year | Median Price (Houston) | Median Price (National) | Houston/National Ratio |
|------|------------------------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| 2010 | $156,000 | $221,000 | 0.71 |
| 2015 | $189,000 | $245,000 | 0.77 |
| 2020 | $248,000 | $322,000 | 0.77 |
| 2024 | $315,000 | $412,000 | 0.76 |

**Real Growth (2010-2024):**
- Houston: +68% nominal, +28% real
- National: +86% nominal, +42% real

### 3.2 Price-to-Income Ratios

Houston has maintained a relatively favorable price-to-income ratio:

| Metropolitan Area | 2010 Ratio | 2024 Ratio | Change |
|-------------------|------------|------------|--------|
| Houston | 3.1 | 3.8 | +0.7 |
| Austin | 3.5 | 5.2 | +1.7 |
| Dallas | 3.2 | 4.1 | +0.9 |
| San Francisco | 7.2 | 9.5 | +2.3 |
| Seattle | 4.8 | 6.9 | +2.1 |
| Phoenix | 3.8 | 4.5 | +0.7 |

Houston's ratio remains below 4.0, generally considered the threshold for moderate affordability, while many peer cities have exceeded 5.0.

### 3.3 Housing Supply Response

Houston's housing market shows robust supply elasticity:

**Annual Housing Permits (2010-2024 Average):**
- Single-Family: 24,600 units/year
- Multi-Family: 18,200 units/year
- Total: 42,800 units/year

**Supply Elasticity Estimate:** 2.1

This elasticity estimate indicates that a 1% increase in housing prices leads to approximately a 2.1% increase in new housing supply, significantly higher than the national average of 0.9.

### 3.4 Rental Market Trends

The rental market tells a similar story:

- Median rent growth: +52% nominal (2010-2024), +16% real
- Rental vacancy rate: 8.2% (2024), above national average of 6.4%
- Share of rent-burdened households: 42% (2024), below peer cities

## 4. Supply and Demand Analysis

### 4.1 Demand Factors

Several factors drive housing demand in Houston:

**Population Growth:**
- Metro population increased from 5.9M (2010) to 7.5M (2024)
- Average annual growth: 1.9%
- Both domestic migration and international immigration contribute

**Economic Growth:**
- Real GDP growth averaging 2.4% annually
- Employment growth of 1.6M jobs (2010-2024)
- Median household income growth: +38% nominal

**Demographic Shifts:**
- Increasing household formation among millennials
- Growing Hispanic population (now 44% of metro area)
- Increasing single-person households

### 4.2 Supply Factors

Houston's strong supply response reflects several characteristics:

**Land Availability:**
- Metro area size: 10,000+ square miles
- Low topographic constraints
- Extensive road infrastructure enabling sprawl

**Regulatory Environment:**
- Absence of traditional zoning
- Private deed restrictions instead of public regulation
- Streamlined permitting processes
- Lower development impact fees than peer cities

**Construction Sector:**
- Large, competitive homebuilding industry
- Over 200 active residential developers
- Integrated supply chains

## 5. Preliminary Findings

This first installment establishes several key findings:

1. **Relative Affordability Maintained**: Despite strong demand, Houston has maintained better affordability than most peer cities

2. **Elastic Supply**: Houston's housing supply responds strongly to price signals, helping moderate price increases

3. **Diverse Product Mix**: Both single-family and multi-family construction contribute to supply

4. **Geographic Expansion**: Outward growth continues to accommodate population increases

5. **Rental Market Flexibility**: Relatively high vacancy rates indicate supply keeps pace with demand

## 7. Conclusion

This first installment establishes that Houston has maintained relatively affordable housing despite strong population and economic growth. The city's elastic housing supply appears to be a key factor, enabled by abundant land and a permissive regulatory environment.

However, important questions remain: Is Houston's affordability sustainable? What are the trade-offs of the current development model? How can policy support affordability while addressing environmental concerns and infrastructure needs? Subsequent installments will address these questions.
